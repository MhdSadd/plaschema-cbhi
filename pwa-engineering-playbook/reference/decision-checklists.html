Operational reference

Decision checklists

Copy these questions into a design review. Do not begin transactional offline work until the answers are clear.

Plan

Product promise

  • User and field job named.
  • Preparation step named.
  • Offline duration and data volume estimated.
  • Unavailable offline actions stated.
  • Harm from loss, exposure, duplication, and staleness ranked.

Architecture

  • Capability level selected.
  • Every data source has one owner.
  • Cache Storage and IndexedDB roles separated.
  • Local tables, indexes, transactions, and migrations designed.
  • Backend idempotency and conflict contracts confirmed.

Lifecycle

  • Service-worker scope and update policy chosen.
  • Old page/new worker/new database compatibility defined.
  • Retention, logout, wipe, and device handover defined.
  • Rollback after database upgrade designed.

Quality

  • Target browsers and installed/tab modes named.
  • Real-device and interruption scenarios written.
  • Privacy-safe events and support view defined.
  • Launch and incident owners named.

Build in safe slices

  1. Responsive online foundation and typed transport.
  2. Manifest, shell precache, navigation fallback, and update prompt.
  3. IndexedDB schema, migration fixtures, capacity handling, and live queries.
  4. Offline reference download with metadata and atomic replacement.
  5. Local-first form and draft-to-outbox transaction.
  6. Idempotent server mutation and durable queue state machine.
  7. File upload stages and orphan cleanup.
  8. Offline session, expiry, access change, logout, and wipe behavior.
  9. Error UX, health view, privacy-safe observability, and recovery.
  10. Browser, real-device, deployment, update, migration, and rollback drills.

Rank findings: critical for likely data loss, exposure, or duplicate harm; high for broken core offline promises; medium for degraded recovery or operations; low for clarity and maintainability.
